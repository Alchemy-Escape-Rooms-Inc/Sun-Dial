Earlier this session we briefly tested an alternative approach
(local edit to Sand_dial_new_boards_FINAL.ino removing per-symbol
digitalWrite(HOUSE_n, LOW) so pins latched HIGH instead of pulsing)
plus FW 3.2.0 with INPUT_PULLDOWN, debounce, and a wait-for-LOW
re-arm. That work was not flashed. User decided to revert to the
original LOW->HIGH->LOW pulse semantics and instead fix the bridge
so it cannot miss any pulse.

Hypothesis for why pin 5 specifically is missed:
  Bridge loop() called ensureWiFi() and ensureMqtt() at the top of
  every iteration. If WiFi or MQTT hiccups during the 2 s HIGH
  window of any single correct symbol, the bridge would return from
  ensureMqtt() (up to 3 s blocking in FW 3.1.0) AFTER the pin had
  already gone LOW, so the rising edge was simply never sampled.
  The 5th correct symbol is statistically the one most likely to
  hit one of those windows after the previous four have fired.

      - ensureMqtt no longer sleeps 3 s on failure; it just retries
        on the next loop pass so we keep servicing pin events.
      - On SOLVED, after publishing, the bridge waits up to 8 s for
        all pins to read LOW before re-arming, so a stale HIGH
        cannot immediately re-latch a pin in the next round.
      - Order-independent counting and per-pin dedupe preserved
        from FW 3.1.0.

GPIO15 ESP32-S3 research (corrects earlier note about strapping):
  - GPIO15 is NOT a strapping pin on the ESP32-S3. Strapping pins
    on the S3 are GPIO 0, 3, 45, 46. GPIO15 was a strapping pin
    (MTDO / ROM debug) on the original ESP32 only.
  - GPIO15 outputs LOW for ~60 us during power-up (chip-level
    glitch). Harmless for our case since we are reading, not
    driving.
  - GPIO15 is ADC2_CH4. ADC2 conflicts with WiFi, but ONLY for
    analog reads. digitalRead / attachInterrupt are unaffected.
  - GPIO15 is not USB or JTAG on the S3.
  - Therefore GPIO15 is electrically fine for INPUT_PULLDOWN +
    RISING-edge interrupts with WiFi running.
  - Remaining hardware suspects for the missing 5th pin (in order):
      1. board-specific routing — check the dev board schematic
         for anything tied to GPIO15 (LED, divider, shared net).
      2. level shifter / divider on the wire to GPIO15.
      3. damaged input from prolonged 5 V abuse — easy test:
         physically swap the GPIO15 wire with a known-good pin
         (e.g. GPIO16 or GPIO17) and update HOUSE_PINS[] to match.
         If the previously-working pin now misses, GPIO15 is fine
         and the issue is upstream wiring. If GPIO15 still misses,
         the input is damaged.
